The conversation ends before the work begins

Customers ask the same questions every day, often after the person who knows the answer has moved on to something else. Each new message arrives without the history of who they are, what they asked, and which rule applies. The team sends a familiar paragraph, and the booking or the follow-up still has to happen in another system. The conversation and the work never meet.

A chat window can talk. It does not know this customer, and it cannot finish the request in the system the team already uses. When it guesses, the customer is the one who discovers the mistake. The assistant that belongs here answers from the rules, completes the request, and brings a person in when the question leaves that ground.

What the system delivers

  • A conversation that continues

    The next reply already knows what this customer said. Another person's history stays out of the thread.

  • A request that is finished

    A booking, a status, or a callback is completed in the system the team already uses. Anything else waits for a person.

  • A handoff with the context

    When the question sits outside the rules, a person receives the thread and the facts already gathered.

  • A record of the work

    The action, the rule it followed, and the moment it stopped for a person remain visible afterward.

Where it earns its place

  • After-hours questions

    The assistant answers the questions the company has already written down. Everything else waits until morning, with the thread attached.

  • Status on an open request

    It reads the record the company already keeps and says where the work stands, so the team is not hunting a second inbox for the same fact.

  • A routine request, completed

    A booking, a callback, or a form is completed in the system the team already uses. The customer is not asked to start the request again.

A small job, inside a limit

Safe code execution ships inside this assistant. A job already on the list can run inside a limit set in advance: a calculation, a file check, a draft. The rest of the business stays outside that limit. Read how the limit works

From the operation to a live system

Every service follows the same path, from the operation the company already runs to a system that stays live.

  1. Plan

    The job, the rules, and the moments that belong to a person are settled before anything is built, so the system has one clear picture of the business.

  2. Build

    The system is built on Cloudflare and connected to the records the company already keeps, so the work continues in the tools the team already trusts.

  3. Review

    Last week's real work is the test. The team decides what is ready for customers, and a customer-facing step does not go live on the studio's judgment alone.

  4. Launch and run

    The system goes live with the first runs watched, the rules left where the team can change them, and the delivery already in the company's hands.

Questions

Will it invent an answer?

It answers from the rules and the records it is given. When those do not cover the question, it says so and hands the conversation to a person.

Can it take actions, or only chat?

Both, within the jobs on the list. A booking or a status update is an action. Anything outside that list waits for a person.

Does it remember an earlier conversation?

Yes, for the customer or the thread that is connected. A new person does not see someone else's history.

What has to be maintained?

The rules and the facts. When a policy or a price changes, that source is updated, and the assistant follows the new version.

Can it email a customer on its own?

Only for the messages on the list, and only after the team has reviewed that step. A surprise message is a failed design.
