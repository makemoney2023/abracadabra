The shift

Smart assistants that can’t see your business.

Your AI assistant doesn’t know your customers. It can’t check your calendar. It can’t look up a job. So someone copies and pastes, and the answer is only as good as what got pasted.

We fix that with MCP, short for Model Context Protocol. It is an open standard, now looked after by the Linux Foundation, that lets an AI assistant plug into your tools through one common connector. It works with Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Cursor and more. We build it on Cloudflare, so it’s fast, secure and ready to grow.

The outcome

What you get

  • Ask your business

    Your team asks plain questions about your CRM, jobs or inventory and gets real answers. Read-only to start.

  • Let your AI do the busywork

    Tools that book appointments, log calls, draft invoices or update a job. Each one asks for a yes before it acts.

  • One safe front door

    All your AI connections behind one sign-in, with rules for who can use what, and a record of every action.

  • Build it once

    One connector works across the AI tools your team uses. If you switch AI tools later, the connector comes with you.

Where it earns its keep

Use cases

  • A question you ask every day

    “What’s booked for Friday?” “Which quotes are still open?” The assistant reads the answer from your tools. It can’t change anything.

  • A routine update

    “Log this call and set a follow-up for Thursday.” The assistant shows you the change and saves it after you say yes.

  • A job with a few steps

    Find who is free after 2pm, suggest who takes the job, and book it once a person confirms.

Questions, answered

FAQ

faq-title

What is MCP?

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol. It is an open standard that lets an AI assistant plug into your tools through one common connector, instead of a custom link for each AI tool.

Which AI tools does it work with?

Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Cursor and more. Some features depend on your plan with that AI company. We check yours before we build.

Can the AI change things on its own?

Only what you allow. We start read-only. Anything that changes a record, sends a message or spends money asks a person for a yes first.

Do we need a connector if our tool already has one?

Maybe not. Many tools now offer their own. We connect to those, and only build what is missing.

Is it safe?

We keep each tool’s access narrow, treat what the AI reads as untrusted, and log every action. Permissions are checked by the connector, not left to the AI.
