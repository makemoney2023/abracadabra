The summary arrives without a source

Someone pastes a paragraph before a decision. It sounds current. It has no link. The person who has to own the decision either trusts it or spends an hour opening tabs to see whether it is real. Both are bad. Trusting it is how a stale page becomes company fact. Rechecking it by hand is the job the tool was supposed to do.

A chatbot will still answer when it did not open a page. That is the wrong product for a question that has to be current. Your own documents are the wrong pile too, when the fact lives outside the company.

What changes

  • The links stay attached

    A claim you are meant to trust carries a page. You can open it. A sentence with no page is not a finding.

  • A refusal when the web is thin

    If the search does not support the answer, the brief says so. It does not smooth the gap into a paragraph.

  • Public pages only

    It reads what a stranger could open. A page behind a login is out of scope, on purpose.

  • A brief you can keep

    You can save the one you want. The saved copy still has the links. It is a start, not a source.

Three places it shows up

  • A claim checked against this month

    You ask whether a public statement is still true. The brief shows the pages, including one that contradicts it if that page exists.

  • A short brief before a meeting

    The question is specific. The reply is short, with the links under it, so the room can open the one that matters.

  • Two public sources, side by side

    You ask for the disagreement, not a blend. The brief keeps the two pages apart and says where they differ.

How it gets built

The order is the same on every service. The job is what changes.

  1. Brief

    You tell us the job, the rules, and the moment a person has to say yes. We write that list before anyone builds. A step that is not on the list does not ship.

  2. Build

    We build it on Cloudflare and connect it to the records you already keep. The new system should not be a second place to type the same fact.

  3. People approve

    Your team runs last week's real work through it and marks what may go live. We do not put a customer-facing step in front of people on our say-so alone.

  4. Launch and run

    It goes live. We watch the first runs with you, fix what the real work shows, and leave the rules where your team can change them.

Questions

How is this different from asking a chatbot?

The answer has to point at pages. If it cannot find a page, it says so. Fluency is not a source.

Can it read pages behind a login?

No. It reads public pages. Private files belong in answers from your documents, not here.

Will it cite a page it did not open?

No. Every claim you are meant to trust carries a link you can open. A remembered citation is not good enough.

Is the brief advice?

It is a reading list with a summary. The decision stays with you. We do not dress a source list up as a recommendation to buy, sue, or treat.

How current is it?

As current as the pages it opened for that run. A saved brief is frozen on that day. Ask again if the week matters.

Can this replace someone who knows the field?

No. It shortens the reading. A person who knows the field still decides what the pages mean.
