The real job lives in a side sheet

The rented app has the right nouns and the wrong order. People export, fix the row in a sheet, and import, or they keep the sheet and let the app go stale. A new hire learns the sheet, not the product you pay for. The record of the business is whichever file was saved last.

Buying another module adds a login. Hiring a developer without a written job adds a queue. The missing piece is usually small and specific: the estimate, the booking, the record the team actually trusts, the public page a buyer reads before they ask.

What changes

  • A screen in the real order

    The first thing the team does in the morning is the first thing on the screen. Extra fields that nobody uses are not there.

  • One record

    The customer, the job, and the number live in one place. A side sheet is a sign the app is not done.

  • A public page when the job needs one

    If a buyer or a caller has to understand the offer before they talk to you, that page is part of the app, not a separate project that drifts.

  • An exit

    You can export the record. We do not keep it in a format you cannot leave.

Three places it shows up

  • The record the team works in

    Customers, jobs, and the numbers, in the order the day actually happens. People stop reconciling two tools at the end of the week.

  • A request that should become work

    An estimate, a booking, or a form on the public site lands in the record. Nobody retypes it to make it real.

  • A page a buyer can finish

    The offer, the comparison, and the questions are on the page. The buyer shows up having read them, and the team is not repeating the same explanation.

How it gets built

The order is the same on every service. The job is what changes.

  1. Brief

    You tell us the job, the rules, and the moment a person has to say yes. We write that list before anyone builds. A step that is not on the list does not ship.

  2. Build

    We build it on Cloudflare and connect it to the records you already keep. The new system should not be a second place to type the same fact.

  3. People approve

    Your team runs last week's real work through it and marks what may go live. We do not put a customer-facing step in front of people on our say-so alone.

  4. Launch and run

    It goes live. We watch the first runs with you, fix what the real work shows, and leave the rules where your team can change them.
