AI media: images, video, transcripts

What it is

Help making the pictures, the short clips, and the transcripts the business already needs. A person still approves what gets published. The model drafts. You decide.

What it looks like in your business

  • A first draft of an image for a page you are about to publish
  • A transcript of a call or a recording the team needs to read
  • A short clip cut from footage you already shot, with the words on screen you supply

How it works

  1. You tell us the asset and the rule: what it is for, and what must not appear.
  2. A draft is made on Cloudflare from your prompt or your file.
  3. You approve, reject, or ask for another pass before anything is public.
  4. The approved file is stored where the rest of the site or the team can use it.

What you get

  • Drafts of images, transcripts, or short video from your own inputs
  • An approval step before anything is published
  • The finished file, in a format the site or the team can use
  • A record of which draft you approved

Who it's for

Owners who need more visuals or transcripts than the team can make by hand, and who still want a person on the publish button.

Signs you need it

  • Pages go out with no picture because nobody had time
  • Recordings sit unwatched because nobody transcribed them
  • You want drafts fast and a clear yes before they are public

Built on Workers AI, Images, Stream. Built and deployed on Cloudflare's global network.

Questions

Will you publish pictures of our customers?

No, unless you give us that photo and the right to use it. Drafts start from your prompt or your file.

Are the images ours to use?

You get the file we made for you, for the use we agreed. We do not resell it.

Can the text inside an image be trusted?

Short labels are checked by a person before publish. If a line must be exact, we set it as text on the page rather than hoping the picture spells it.

Do transcripts replace the recording?

They are a reading copy. The recording stays the source if the words matter.
