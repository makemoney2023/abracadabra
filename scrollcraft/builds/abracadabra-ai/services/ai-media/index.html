The draft is late, so the page goes out bare

A page, a post, or a follow-up needs an image or a short clip, and the person who makes them is on something else. The page ships with a gray box, or it waits. Recordings pile up because nobody has time to write down what was said. The words the business already approved never make it onto the asset.

A generated picture that ignores the brief is worse than no picture. Wrong claims, wrong labels, a face you do not have the right to use. Speed without an approval step publishes the mistake.

What changes

  • A draft from your inputs

    The image, the transcript, or the clip starts from the brief, the file, or the footage you supplied. It does not start from a guess about your brand.

  • Words you can check

    Short labels are read by a person before they are public. A line that must be exact is set as text, not left to the picture to spell.

  • A yes before it is public

    Nothing is posted, mailed, or placed on a page until a person you name approves that file.

  • The file, where the team can use it

    The approved asset is stored with the record of which draft you picked, in a format the site or the post can use.

Three places it shows up

  • A picture for a page you are about to publish

    You describe the shot and the words that must appear. You get drafts. You pick one, or you reject the set. The page does not invent its own.

  • A transcript of a recording the team needs to read

    The recording stays the source. The transcript is a reading copy a person can correct before anyone quotes it.

  • A short clip from footage you already shot

    The cut uses your footage and the line you supplied. It is not a new scene starring people you did not film.

How it gets built

The order is the same on every service. The job is what changes.

  1. Brief

    You tell us the job, the rules, and the moment a person has to say yes. We write that list before anyone builds. A step that is not on the list does not ship.

  2. Build

    We build it on Cloudflare and connect it to the records you already keep. The new system should not be a second place to type the same fact.

  3. People approve

    Your team runs last week's real work through it and marks what may go live. We do not put a customer-facing step in front of people on our say-so alone.

  4. Launch and run

    It goes live. We watch the first runs with you, fix what the real work shows, and leave the rules where your team can change them.

Questions

Will you publish a picture of a customer?

No, unless you give us that photo and the right to use it. Drafts start from your prompt, your file, or your footage.

Are the files ours to use?

You get the file we made for the use we agreed. We do not resell it and we do not drop it into another client's work.

Can the text inside an image be trusted?

No, not by default. A person checks short labels. If a line must be exact, we set it as text on the page.

Do transcripts replace the recording?

They are a reading copy. If the words matter, the recording stays the source and the transcript is corrected against it.

Can it match a face from a photo we found online?

No. We do not build a person's likeness from a picture you do not have rights to.

What if we hate the whole set?

Then nothing ships. Rejection is a normal outcome of the approval step, not a failed project.
