AI Agents vs Agentic AI: A Plain Split

An agent takes a goal and chooses tools. Agentic AI is the wider bet, and many of those projects are forecast to be canceled. Start with a workflow.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

An AI agent, in Anthropic's December 2024 essay, is a system that directs its own tools toward a goal.1 A workflow is the opposite: the steps are written in advance. Agentic AI is the category vendors use when they mean many such systems, often across a company. The words are not interchangeable.

The definition is the short version. The jobs list is where an agent might sit. The people guide is who has to check the output.

What the forecast says

Gartner said in June 2025 that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027.2 The reasons are cost, unclear business value, and inadequate risk controls. The same note said 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously by 2028, up from 0% in 2024, and that 33% of enterprise software will include agentic AI by 2028, up from less than 1% in 2024.

If you cannot name the checker, you do not have an agent yet.

Run the Readiness Check

If you can write the steps, you want a workflow. If the next step depends on what the last step found, you may want an agent, and you still want a person who can stop it. Calling that agentic does not change the checkpoint.

Questions

What is the difference between an agent and a workflow?

A workflow has its steps written in advance. An agent chooses tools as it goes. Anthropic says to start with the simplest of the two.

What does the agentic forecast add?

Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be canceled by the end of 2027, for cost, unclear value, or inadequate risk controls.

Does a canceled forecast mean you should avoid agents?

It means you should name the job and the person who checks it. A workflow that already works does not need the extra risk.
