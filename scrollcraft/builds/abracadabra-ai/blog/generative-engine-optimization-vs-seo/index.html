Generative Engine Optimization vs SEO

Google says the new names are still SEO. A research paper measures visibility. Pew measured the click. Write the page so both can use it.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

Generative engine optimization and SEO are not rival crafts in Google's guide. Google says answer-engine optimization and generative-engine optimization are still SEO.1 The page must be indexed and eligible for a snippet. Google does not use llms.txt. There is no special chunking requirement.

The visibility guide is the check. The answer-engine note is the tactic list. The llms.txt note is the file Google says it ignores.

What changes, and what does not

The 2024 paper reports visibility gains of up to 40% when the page is written for a generative answer.2 Citations, quotations, and statistics were associated with gains over 40%. Perplexity showed gains up to 37%. Position-adjusted word count rose 41%, and subjective impression rose 28%, on a benchmark of 10,000 queries.

Pew found, in July 2025, that a visit with an AI summary was followed by a click 8% of the time, against 15% with no summary.3 SEO still gets the page indexed. The generative layer still has to be worth the click.

Questions

Does Google treat generative engine optimization as separate from SEO?

No. Google's guide says answer-engine optimization and generative-engine optimization are still SEO, and that Google does not use llms.txt.

What did the research paper measure?

The 2024 paper reports visibility gains of up to 40%, up to 37% on Perplexity, and a benchmark of 10,000 queries.

What happens to the click when a summary appears?

Pew found, in July 2025, a click 8% of the time with an AI summary, against 15% when no summary appeared.
