Why AI Pilots Stall After the Demo

Pilots stall when the data, the risk controls, or the value was never named. Surveys show use is common and scaling is rare. The demo is not the process.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

An AI pilot stalls when the demo worked and the process did not. Gartner's January 2026 article says at least 50% of generative AI projects were abandoned after proof of concept, because of poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs, or unclear value.1

The decision guide is the matching fix. The implementation framework is the sequence. The data note is the first failure.

Use is not scaling

McKinsey's 2025 survey says 88% of respondents report regular AI use in at least one function, up from 78% a year earlier.2 About one third of organizations are scaling. 23% are scaling an agentic system somewhere, and 39% are experimenting. No more than 10% are scaling agents in any one function. 29% of firms under $100 million in revenue are at the scaling stage, against nearly half of firms over $5 billion.

Gartner said in June 2025 that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027.3

A stalled pilot is a decision. Score it before you fund another demo.

Run the Readiness Check

A pilot that cannot name the data, the checker, and the value is already in the abandoned pile. Move it into a written process or stop it.

Questions

Why do proofs of concept get abandoned?

Gartner's 2026 article names poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs, and unclear value, and says at least 50% were abandoned after proof of concept.

Is regular use the same as scaling?

No. McKinsey's 2025 survey says 88% report regular use, while 23% are scaling an agentic system and no more than 10% scale agents in any one function.

What should you do with a stalled pilot?

Name the data, the person who checks the output, and the value. If you cannot, stop the pilot.
