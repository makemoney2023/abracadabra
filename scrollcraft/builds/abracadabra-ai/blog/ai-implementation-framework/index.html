An AI Implementation Framework in Five Stages

Name the job, score the data, write the process, assign a checker, then keep or stop. Surveys show use is common and scaling is not.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

An implementation framework is the order of work after you know the job. NIST names Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage.1 This note uses five stages in words: name the job, score the data, write the process, assign a checker, then keep or stop.

The Forming band is where many businesses sit while this is unfinished. The stall note is what happens if you skip a stage. The readiness framework is the score that comes first.

What the numbers say about the middle

McKinsey's 2025 survey says 88% report regular AI use in at least one function.2 23% are scaling an agentic system somewhere, and 39% are experimenting. 29% of firms under $100 million are scaling, against nearly half of firms over $5 billion. No more than 10% scale agents in any one function.

Cisco's 2025 survey of 8,000 leaders found pacesetters, near 13% of that sample, were 4 times more likely to move a pilot into production.3 Those leaders work in organizations with more than 500 employees. A small business can still copy the sequence: a written process, then production.
