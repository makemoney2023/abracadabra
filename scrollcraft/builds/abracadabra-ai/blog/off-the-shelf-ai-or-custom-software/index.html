Off-the-Shelf AI or Custom Software

Buy the product when it already does the job. Build only when the job is yours alone. Surveys show generative AI use is already common among small firms.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

Off-the-shelf AI is the right buy when a product already does the job. Custom software is the right build when the job is specific to how this business works, and no product covers it. The studio takes the work that products do not cover. The consultant note is what to ask before either path. The agency comparison is who you hire.

What the surveys count

The Chamber's 2025 report says 58% of small businesses use generative AI, up from 40% in 2024 and 23% in 2023.1 That is a use claim. It is not a claim that those firms built custom software.

Salesforce surveyed 3,350 leaders at firms of 200 employees and under.2 75% were at least experimenting. 83% of growing small and midsize businesses said that. 91% of those already using AI said it boosts revenue. The survey does not say whether the tool was bought or built.

If a product already does the job, the check will not tell you to build it.

Run the Readiness Check

If a bought product covers the job, buy it. If the job depends on your records, your exceptions, and your checker, the product will not invent those. That is the custom case.

Questions

When should you buy off the shelf?

When a product already does the job and you can name who checks the output. A custom build for a solved job wastes the budget.

When is custom software the right path?

When the job depends on records and exceptions a product does not cover, and you can still name the checker.

Does generative AI use mean firms built their own tools?

No. The Chamber's 2025 figure, 58%, counts use. It does not count custom builds.
