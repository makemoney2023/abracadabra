AI Readiness Assessment Questionnaire: The Themes

Ask about data, process, people, and the decision before you ask about a tool. A public checklist does not publish a readiness rate for small firms.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

An AI readiness questionnaire should force four themes into the open: where the data lives, whether the process is written, who checks the output, and what decision success means. NIST groups the risk work as Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage.1 A questionnaire that starts with a product name has skipped those themes.

The framework is how to score the answers. The assessment is the longer reading. The growth guide is what the check does after the score.

What a public checklist does not claim

The AWS checklist for small and midsize businesses, updated in September 2025, walks an owner through readiness questions.2 It does not publish a percentage of businesses that are ready. The OECD SME AI adoption tool, current in 2026, is a self-assessment aid for smaller firms.3 It is not a national adoption rate.

Themes are not a score. The Readiness Check returns the band.

Run the Readiness Check

Ask where the record of truth lives. Ask which step is written and which step is habit. Ask who can reject the output. Ask what you will stop doing if the answer is weak. Those four questions are the questionnaire.

Questions

What themes belong on the questionnaire?

Data, process, people, and the decision. A tool question comes after those four themes are answered.

Does the AWS checklist publish a readiness percentage?

No. The September 2025 page is a checklist for small and midsize businesses. It does not cite a share of businesses that are ready.

Is the OECD tool an adoption rate?

No. The tool, current in 2026, is a self-assessment aid for smaller firms. It is not a statistic for a country.
