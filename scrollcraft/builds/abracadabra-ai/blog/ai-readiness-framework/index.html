An AI Readiness Framework an Owner Can Score

Score data, process, people, and the decision in words, then read the band. Public research names the gaps. The check names the one that is yours.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

An AI readiness framework is a way to score the business before you score a tool. NIST groups AI risk work as Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage.1 This note uses four groups an owner can see: data, process, people, and the decision. Each group is absent, partial, in place, or running. Those words are anchors. They are not a percentage.

The readiness guide is the matching page. The assessment is the longer note. The questionnaire is the question list.

What the bands mean

The Readiness Check scores 0 to 39 as Early, 40 to 59 as Forming, 60 to 79 as Ready, and 80 to 100 as Running.2 The check educates and routes. It does not certify a business.

Gartner said in February 2025 that 63% of respondents do not have, or are unsure they have, the data management practices AI needs.3 Through 2026, organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects that lack AI-ready data. A framework that scores data as running while the source of truth is a shared inbox is scoring a wish.

Cisco's 2025 index surveyed 8,000 senior leaders in organizations with more than 500 employees.4 Pacesetters stayed near 13%. Those organizations were 4 times more likely to move a pilot into production. That sample is not a small business. The pattern still matters: production follows a written process, not a demo.

Questions

How do you score a group without a fake percentage?

Call it absent, partial, in place, or running. Do not invent a percentage for a group you have not measured.

What are the Readiness Check bands?
