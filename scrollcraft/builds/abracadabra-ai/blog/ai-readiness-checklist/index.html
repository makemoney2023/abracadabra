AI Readiness Checklist Before You Buy a Tool

Questions to answer before you buy an AI tool, grouped by data, process, people, and the decision. Public surveys name the gaps those questions catch.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

An AI readiness checklist is the list you finish before you pay for a tool. This note groups it into data, process, people, and the decision. The Readiness Check scores the same four. The assessment note compares the public tools. The data note stays on the first group. The process guide is the matching fix when that score is low.

Data and process

Can you name the source of truth for the job? Is it current, and can a system read it without someone retyping it? Is the job written down, including the exceptions, and does one person own the handoff?

Gartner reported in February 2025 that 63% of respondents do not have, or are unsure they have, the data management practices AI needs.1 The same note says that through 2026 organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects that lack AI-ready data.

AWS published a checklist in September 2025.2 It walks five steps and sorts a business into four stages: Not yet ready, Emerging, Operational, and Scaling. The page does not cite an adoption percentage.

People and the decision

Who may use a tool, and who may send its output to a customer? Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index says 75% of knowledge workers use AI at work, and 46% of those users started less than six months earlier.3 78% of AI users bring their own tools. At small and midsize companies that share is 80%. 60% of leaders worry their organization's leadership lacks a plan and vision to implement AI.

The decision questions are separate. What would count as success, who can stop the experiment, and what happens if the tool is wrong?

Questions

What should an AI readiness checklist cover?

Data, the written process, the people who will use the output, and the decision about what success means. A tool purchase is the last step, not the first.

Does the AWS checklist publish an adoption rate?

No. The September 2025 page walks five steps and names four stages. It does not cite a percentage of businesses that are ready.

Why ask who brings their own AI tool?

Microsoft's 2024 index says 78% of AI users bring their own tools, and 80% at small and midsize businesses. A checklist that ignores that will miss the tools already in the building.
