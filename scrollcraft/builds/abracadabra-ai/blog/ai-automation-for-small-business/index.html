AI Automation for Small Business: Which Job First

Pick the first job by volume, how stable the rules are, whether the data can be read, and what a wrong answer costs. Surveys show which jobs already appear.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

AI automation for a small business is a choice of job, not a catalog of tools. Score a candidate on four things, in words: how often it happens, how stable the rules are, whether a system can read the data, and what a wrong answer costs. High volume, stable rules, readable data, and a cheap catch belong first. A rare decision with an expensive mistake does not.

The pressure guide asks where the work slows down. The mapping note is the step before a tool. The agency note is the hiring question that comes after.

What the surveys already count

Statistics Canada, for the second quarter of 2025, found that 12.2% of businesses used AI to produce goods or deliver services, up from 6.1% a year earlier.1 Among businesses that used AI, text analytics accounted for 35.7% and marketing automation for 23.1%. 40.1% had developed new workflows, and 38.9% had trained staff.

The Chamber's 2025 report says 58% of small businesses use generative AI, up from 40% in 2024 and 23% in 2023.2 That is a different question. Opening a generative tool is not the same as putting AI inside delivery.

Questions

Which job should a small business automate first?

The one with high volume, stable rules, data a system can read, and a wrong answer that a person can catch cheaply. A rare, high-stakes decision fails that test.

What share of Canadian businesses used AI in production?

In the second quarter of 2025, 12.2% used AI to produce goods or deliver services, up from 6.1% a year earlier.

Is generative-AI use the same as automation?

No. The 2025 Chamber report says 58% of small businesses use generative AI. That question is not the same as AI inside the delivery of the product.
