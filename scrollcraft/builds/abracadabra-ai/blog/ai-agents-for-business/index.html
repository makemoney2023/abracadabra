AI Agents for Business: When to Use One

Eight jobs can sit with an agent only when a person still checks the output. Anthropic says to start simpler. Gartner expects many agent projects to be canceled.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

An agent earns a place in the business only when the job branches and a person still checks the output. Anthropic's December 2024 essay says to start with the simplest solution, and it separates a fixed workflow from an agent that directs its own tools.1 If the steps do not change, write the workflow and stop.

The people guide is the matching fix. The definition is the vocabulary. The agentic comparison is the next distinction.

Eight jobs, each with a stop

Inbox triage waits for a person before anything is sent. Meeting notes wait before they become tasks. Quote follow-up waits before a price goes out. Invoice questions wait before a payment promise. Inventory exceptions wait before a purchase. Support replies wait before they reach the customer. Schedule changes wait before a customer is told. A weekly report waits before it is treated as the record.

Job What the person still does
Inbox triage Decides what is actually sent
Meeting notes Accepts the tasks
Quote follow-up Approves the price
Invoice questions Approves any promise to pay
Inventory exceptions Approves the order
Support replies Approves what the customer sees
Schedule changes Approves the message
Weekly report Accepts it as the record

Gartner said in June 2025 that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027.2 The reasons are cost, unclear value, and inadequate risk controls. A job from the list above without a named checker is a candidate for that pile.

Questions

When should a business use an agent instead of a workflow?

When the next step depends on what the last step found, and you cannot write every branch in advance. Anthropic says to start with the simplest system that works.

What belongs in the human checkpoint?

A named person, a place where the output waits, and a rule for what happens if it is wrong. A checkpoint that nobody owns is not a checkpoint.

Why are so many agent projects expected to stop?
