AI Strategy for Small Business: Start With the Job

Name the job, the data, and who checks the output before you buy a tool. Use is already common. A written plan and staff training are not.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

An AI strategy for a small business is a short list: the job that changes, the data it needs, and the person who checks the output. It is not a tool catalog. The assessment scores the same groups. The implementation framework is the sequence after the job is named. The readiness framework is the scoring sheet.

Use is ahead of the plan

Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index says 75% of knowledge workers use AI at work.1 46% of those users started less than six months before the survey. 60% of leaders worry their organization's leadership lacks a plan and vision to implement AI. 78% of AI users bring their own tools, and that share is 80% at small and midsize businesses. 55% of leaders are concerned about talent. Only 25% of companies were planning to offer generative AI training.

NIST's risk framework groups the work as Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage.2 A strategy that cannot point at those jobs has not started.

Salesforce surveyed 3,350 leaders at firms of 200 employees and under.3 The fieldwork ran through August and September 2024. 75% said they were at least experimenting with AI. Among growing small and midsize businesses, 83% said that. 78% of growing firms planned to increase AI investment, against 55% of firms that were declining. 91% of those already using AI said it boosts revenue.

Those figures describe different populations. Microsoft asked knowledge workers and leaders. Salesforce asked small-business leaders. Neither figure is a substitute for the job you can name on one page.

Questions

What belongs in a small-business AI strategy?

The job that changes, the data it needs, who checks the output, and how you will know it failed. A tool name comes after those answers.

What share of knowledge workers already use AI?

Microsoft's 2024 index says 75% of knowledge workers use AI at work, and 78% of AI users bring their own tools.

Does high use mean the staff were trained?

No. The same Microsoft index says only 25% of companies were planning generative AI training.
