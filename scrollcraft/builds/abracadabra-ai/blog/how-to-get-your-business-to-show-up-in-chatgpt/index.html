How to Show Up in ChatGPT and AI Overviews

Showing up in ChatGPT search is a crawler and indexing problem. Google still treats answer-engine advice as ordinary SEO. Clicks fall when a summary appears.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

Getting a business to show up in ChatGPT search starts with the crawler that search actually uses. OpenAI documents separate crawlers.1 OAI-SearchBot fetches pages for search results. Opting that crawler out keeps the pages out of ChatGPT search answers. GPTBot is the training crawler. ChatGPT-User fetches when a person triggers a look-up, and robots.txt may not apply to it. They are not one switch.

The visibility guide reads the site. The answer-engine note and the generative-engine note stay on the vocabulary.

What Google says to do

Google's guide for generative features says the work is still SEO.2 It says answer-engine optimization and generative-engine optimization are still SEO from Google's point of view. It says Google does not use llms.txt. It says there is no special chunking requirement. The page has to be indexed, and it has to be eligible for a snippet. No extra schema is required for those features.

A page that is blocked, thin, or ineligible for a snippet will not become an overview because a new file was added. Allow the search crawler, keep the page indexable, and write the answer where a person can also read it.

Pew, in July 2025, found that visits with an AI summary produced a click 8% of the time, against 15% when no summary appeared.3 Showing up is not the same as being clicked. Write the page so the citation is worth the click.

Questions

Which crawler does ChatGPT search use?

OpenAI separates OAI-SearchBot, which fetches pages for search, from GPTBot, which is the training crawler, and from ChatGPT-User, which fetches because a person asked. Blocking the training crawler is not the same switch.

Does Google require a special file for AI overviews?

No. Google's guide says answer-engine and generative-engine advice is still SEO, says it does not use llms.txt, and says a page must be indexed and eligible for a snippet.

Do AI summaries change clicks?

Pew's July 2025 study found that 8% of visits with an AI summary produced a click, compared with 15% of visits without one.
