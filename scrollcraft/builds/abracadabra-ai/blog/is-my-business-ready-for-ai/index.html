Is My Business Ready for AI? Signs to Read First

Ready means the data, the process, the people, and the decision can carry a tool. This note reads the Check bands against a large-firm index.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

A business is ready for AI when a named job can move to a tool without inventing the process on the way. The Readiness Check scores that as four bands. Early is 0–39, Forming is 40–59, Ready is 60–79, and Running is 80–100.1 Each sentence on the check describes a state. The check does not diagnose.

The Early band and the Ready band spell those states out. The assessment note compares the public tools.

What a large-firm index is counting

Cisco's 2025 AI Readiness Index surveyed 8,000 senior leaders at organizations with more than 500 employees, across 30 markets and 26 industries.2 Pacesetters stayed near 13% of that sample. Those organizations were 4 times more likely to move a pilot into production. That sample is not a small-business census. A small firm can be Ready on the check and still look nothing like a pacesetter.

The OECD SME AI adoption tool, part of 2026 Group of Seven work, is a short self-check for smaller firms.3 It takes about five minutes, keeps answers in the browser, and does not publish an adoption rate. Use it as a second set of questions, not as a national share.

The bands describe a state. Score your own business.

Run the Readiness Check

Four signs the work can move: the source of truth is named, the steps are written, a person owns the output, and someone can stop the experiment. Four signs it cannot: the knowledge is only in one head, the exceptions are unwritten, nobody may send the output to a customer, and success has no definition.

Questions

What does ready for AI mean here?

It means the data, the process, the people, and the decision are in a state where a tool can do a named job. It is not a claim that the business has bought a product.

Is a low score a failure?

No. Early is 0–39 and Forming is 40–59. Cisco's pacesetters, the firms furthest along in a large-company index, were about 13% of that sample. A low score is a description.

Does the OECD tool publish an adoption rate?

No. The 2026 SME tool is a short self-check. It stores no data and it does not publish a share of firms that are ready.
