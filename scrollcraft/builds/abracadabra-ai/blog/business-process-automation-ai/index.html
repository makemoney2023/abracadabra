Business Process Automation With AI: Write the Job

Write the steps before you add a model. Most knowledge workers already use AI. Few companies were planning training. Many proofs of concept still stop.

By Christopher Buzny · 2026-10-14

Business process automation with AI starts as a written job. Anthropic's December 2024 essay says to start with the simplest solution that works, and to prefer a fixed workflow until the next step actually depends on the last one.1

The process guide is the matching fix. The automation note is which job to pick. The agents note is when a workflow is not enough.

The process is the product

Microsoft's 2024 index says 75% of knowledge workers use AI, and 46% of those users started less than six months earlier.2 60% of leaders worry leadership lacks a plan and vision. 78% of AI users bring their own tools, and 80% do so at small and midsize businesses. 60% of time in the work apps was spent in email, chat, and meetings, and 40% in creation apps. Only 25% of companies were planning generative AI training. 55% of leaders were concerned about talent.

Gartner's January 2026 article says at least 50% of generative AI projects were abandoned after proof of concept by the end of the prior year, citing poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs, and unclear value.3

If the steps are not written, the model cannot follow them.

Run the Readiness Check

Write the steps a person already follows. Mark the step that waits for a judgment. Automate the steps that do not. If every step needs a judgment, you do not have a process yet.

Questions

Should you automate before you write the steps?

No. Anthropic says to start with the simplest system that works. A model on an unwritten job invents the steps.

What share of companies were planning AI training?

Microsoft's 2024 index says only 25% of companies were planning generative AI training, while 75% of knowledge workers already use AI.
