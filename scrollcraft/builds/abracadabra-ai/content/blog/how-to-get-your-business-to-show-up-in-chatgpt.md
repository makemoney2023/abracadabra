Getting a business into ChatGPT search starts with the crawler that search actually uses, which is a different switch from the training crawler. Block the wrong one, or block all of them, and the rest of the writing never gets a chance.

OpenAI documents separate crawlers.[^openai] OAI-SearchBot fetches pages for search results. Opting that crawler out keeps the pages out of ChatGPT search answers. GPTBot is the training crawler. ChatGPT-User fetches when a person triggers a look-up, and robots.txt may not apply to it. They are not one switch. We say that sentence until the owner can repeat it, because the usual robots file treats "bots" as a single mood. Training, search, and a fetch a person just asked for are three different events. A rule written in a hurry will confuse them, and the confusion is silent. The page simply never shows up, and someone blames the copy.

The [visibility guide](https://check.abra-ca-dabra.app/check/guide/visibility) reads the site the way a practical check should, starting from whether a crawler can see the pages at all. The [answer-engine note](/blog/answer-engine-optimization) and the [generative-engine note](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-seo) stay on the vocabulary around Google's summaries. This note stays on the switches, and on the page those switches are supposed to reveal.

## What Google says to do

Google's guide for generative features says the work is still SEO.[^google] It says answer-engine optimization and generative-engine optimization are still SEO from Google's point of view. It says Google does not use llms.txt. It says there is no special chunking requirement. The page has to be indexed, and it has to be eligible for a snippet. No extra schema is required for those features. If you came here only for ChatGPT, do not skip this. A lot of owners want both surfaces, and the Google half of the job is still an ordinary page. A special file will not replace it on either side.

<!--image:2-->

A page that is blocked, thin, or ineligible for a snippet will not become an overview because a new file was added. Allow the search crawler, keep the page indexable, and write the answer where a person can also read it.

We watch this go wrong in a particular order. First the agency blocks every bot "for safety," including the search crawler, and the owner hears that the site is protected. Then someone adds a new file because a thread said the file is how you show up. The page underneath is still a list of cities and a phone number. Nothing in that sequence produces an answer a homeowner can use. The crawler has to be allowed to fetch. The page has to be indexable. The sentences have to be worth repeating. The file is not the sentence.

<!--cta:inline-->

Pew, in July 2025, found that visits with an AI summary produced a click 8% of the time, against 15% when no summary appeared.[^pew] Showing up is not the same as being clicked. Write the page so the citation is worth the click.

That Pew read is about Google summaries, and we still bring it into this conversation. The habit is the same. A generated answer can satisfy the easy part of the question and leave you unvisited. If you win the mention and lose the call, you have done the visible half of the job. The page has to carry the part a summary cannot finish: who rolls the truck, what you will not touch, how a person actually books.

## A worked example

Walk an electrical contractor with us. This is a scene, not a measured result. Mara owns the company. Ben runs the office, the inbox, and the site password. Two techs, Noor and Ellis, do the work. The question homeowners ask is whether someone can look at a panel that trips, and whether that someone is licensed, insured, and willing to say when the job is a utility problem instead of a house problem.

Their current site is a header, three service words, and a contact form that sometimes fails on a phone. Ben once pasted a robots file from a forum because a post said AI companies were copying the web. He did not know he had turned away the crawler that fetches pages for search. Mara then paid for a rewrite that added a file at the root and left the service page as thin as before. They were told this would help them show up. It did not, because the search crawler was still unwelcome, and the page still had nothing a citation could honestly repeat.

Separate the switches the way OpenAI separates them. OAI-SearchBot is the one that has to be allowed if ChatGPT search is supposed to fetch the panel page. Opting that one out keeps the page out of those answers, which is a legitimate choice if Mara does not want to be there, and a mistake if she does. GPTBot is the training crawler, and it is a different decision. Mara can refuse training and still want search. She cannot express that difference with a single "disallow all" line she does not understand. ChatGPT-User is the third case. It fetches because a person asked, and robots.txt may not apply to it. Ben should know that rule exists so he does not promise Mara that a robots file controls every look-up a user can trigger.

The page itself has to survive being read by a person standing in a dark kitchen. Opening lines answer the panel question. What you do on a trip. What you will not do, including anything on the utility side of the meter. How to reach Ben, and what to say so the right tech brings the right tester. Noor and Ellis should recognize the description, because they are the ones who will hear "but your website said" in a hallway. Mara signs the claims about license and insurance. Ben signs the crawler rules, in writing, with the three bots named in words he can point at.

A generated answer might then mention the firm for a tripping panel in their towns. That mention is not the booking. Pew is the reminder, even though it measured a different summary. When a summary is present, a click is less common. So the paragraph the answer cannot replace is the one about the utility boundary and about calling Ben with a photo of the panel label. The citation gets them interested. The specific refusal gets them to the phone. A thin page gets them a sentence that could have been any electrician in the county.

## How we'd run it

We would touch the robots file and the service page in the same week, and we would not call a new root file the project.

First, write down the three crawlers in Ben's notebook, in plain words. OAI-SearchBot fetches pages for search. Leaving it blocked keeps you out of ChatGPT search answers. GPTBot is training. ChatGPT-User fetches when a person triggers a look-up, and a robots file may not govern it. If Ben cannot say those apart, nobody edits the file yet.

Second, decide the training question on purpose. Some owners want no training crawl. That is allowed, and it is not the same lever as search. Make the search choice second, out loud. If Mara wants to be findable in ChatGPT search, OAI-SearchBot has to be allowed to fetch the pages she hopes will be cited. Write the decision next to the file so the next freelancer does not "clean it up."

Third, look at the Google floor on the same pages. Indexed. Eligible for a snippet. Not blocked, not thin, not hiding the only useful sentence behind a form. Google's guide says the generative features are still SEO, that it does not use llms.txt, and that there is no special chunking requirement. No extra schema is required. A page that fails this floor will not become an overview because a file appeared.

Fourth, rewrite the service page in the voice of the kitchen, not the voice of a keyword list. The panel question, the utility boundary, what to photograph, how booking with Ben works. A person should be able to read it without feeling marketed to. An answer should be able to lift a sentence without inventing the rest.

Fifth, add the reason to click. Assume the summary stole the easy definition of a tripping breaker. Leave the shop-specific part on the page. Who comes. What you refuse. What a homeowner should have ready. Pew, in July 2025, saw a click 8% of the time when a summary appeared, against 15% when none did. Showing up is the citation. The click is a separate piece of writing.

Sixth, test the boring way. Ask someone who does not work there to find the page from the question, on a phone, and to tell you what they would do next. If they cannot tell you, the citation will not save you. Fix the page. Then leave the crawler rules alone unless Mara changes her mind. Stability in the file matters more than a monthly tweak from whoever last read a thread.

## Common mistakes

The first mistake is one switch for every bot. A copied robots file that disallows the world will disallow OAI-SearchBot along with GPTBot. You opted out of search answers and called it privacy. If that was the goal, fine. If the goal was to show up, you closed the door and then paid someone to decorate it.

The second mistake is the magic file. Google says it does not use llms.txt. A blocked, thin, or snippet-ineligible page will not become an overview because a new file was added. We have heard the file sold as the ChatGPT trick too. The trick is the crawler plus a page worth citing. The file is a list, when it is anything.

The third mistake is a page only a model could love. Fragments, repeated town names, no sentence Noor would say in a hallway. There is no special chunking requirement. Headings are for people who scan. If Ellis would be ashamed to read the page to a customer, it is the wrong page, however well a crawler can fetch it.

The fourth mistake is confusing a mention with a call. The July 2025 Pew finding, 8% of visits with a summary producing a click against 15% without one, is the warning. Write the line the summary cannot complete. The boundary of the work. The photo to send. The person who answers.

The fifth mistake is claims nobody will back. "Same hour" when the truck is across the county. "We repair utility equipment" when you do not. ChatGPT-User may fetch the page because a person asked, and robots.txt may not apply, so a reckless sentence can travel even when you thought you had a gate. Write only what Mara will say in the driveway.

The sixth mistake is handing the file to someone who cannot name the bots. Ben owns this, or a person Ben trusts who will explain the diff in words. An anonymous plugin update that "optimizes robots" is how the search crawler disappears again on a Friday.

## What we'd do

We would allow the search crawler, make a separate and explicit choice about training, and spend the real effort on the panel page a homeowner can use. We would not start with a root file, and we would not let a single disallow line stand in for a decision. Our opinion is that most "show up in ChatGPT" projects are a robots accident plus a thin page, wearing a strategy name.

Google still wants an indexed page a snippet can lift, and it still declines the special file and the special schema. OpenAI still uses different crawlers for search, training, and a person-triggered fetch. Pew still says the click thins out when a summary is already on screen. Those three facts are the whole job. Let the search crawler in if you want to be cited. Write the answer where Ben, Noor, and the homeowner would all recognize it. Then make the next step obvious enough that a citation has somewhere to go.
