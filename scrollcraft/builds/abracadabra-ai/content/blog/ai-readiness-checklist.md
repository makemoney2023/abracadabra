An AI readiness checklist is the list you finish before you pay for a tool. This note groups it into data, process, people, and the decision. The [Readiness Check](https://check.abra-ca-dabra.app/check) scores the same four. The [assessment note](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment) compares the public tools. The [data note](/blog/ai-ready-data) stays on the first group. The [process guide](https://check.abra-ca-dabra.app/check/guide/fix/ready-process-low) is the matching fix when that score is low.

## Data and process

Can you name the source of truth for the job? Is it current, and can a system read it without someone retyping it? Is the job written down, including the exceptions, and does one person own the handoff?

Gartner reported in February 2025 that 63% of respondents do not have, or are unsure they have, the data management practices AI needs.[^gartner-data] The same note says that through 2026 organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects that lack AI-ready data.

AWS published a checklist in September 2025.[^aws] It walks five steps and sorts a business into four stages: Not yet ready, Emerging, Operational, and Scaling. The page does not cite an adoption percentage.

## People and the decision

Who may use a tool, and who may send its output to a customer? Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index says 75% of knowledge workers use AI at work, and 46% of those users started less than six months earlier.[^microsoft] 78% of AI users bring their own tools. At small and midsize companies that share is 80%. 60% of leaders worry their organization's leadership lacks a plan and vision to implement AI.

The decision questions are separate. What would count as success, who can stop the experiment, and what happens if the tool is wrong?

<!--cta:inline-->

A tick list does not say which gap is largest. The Readiness Check turns the same four groups into a band.
