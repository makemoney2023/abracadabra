How to use AI in a business is a job list, not a model list. Statistics Canada's 2025 brief asked businesses that already use AI which tools they use and what they changed.[^statcan] Among those businesses, text analytics accounted for 35.7%, and marketing automation for 23.1%. 40.1% had developed new workflows, and 38.9% had trained staff. In the same period, 12.2% of businesses used AI to produce goods or deliver services, up from 6.1% a year earlier.

The [help note](/blog/how-can-ai-help-small-businesses) separates that production question from generative-AI use. The [automation note](/blog/ai-automation-for-small-business) is how to pick the first job. The [agents note](/blog/ai-agents-for-business) is when the steps branch.

## Twelve jobs you can write down

Draft the customer reply, then a person sends it. Summarize the meeting, then a person accepts the tasks. Turn notes into a quote, then a person sets the price. Sort the inbox, then a person decides what is answered. Flag the late invoice, then a person decides the chase. Describe the product, then a person approves the page. Group support tickets, then a person picks the answer. Check the inventory exception, then a person orders. Rewrite the policy in plain language, then a person publishes it. Compare two supplier notes, then a person chooses. Draft the weekly report, then a person accepts the numbers. Prepare the staff answer, then a person trains the team.
