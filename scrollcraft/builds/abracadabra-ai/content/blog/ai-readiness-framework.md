An AI readiness framework is a way to score the business before you score a tool. NIST groups AI risk work as Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage.[^nist] This note uses four groups an owner can see: data, process, people, and the decision. Each group is absent, partial, in place, or running. Those words are anchors. They are not a percentage.

The [readiness guide](https://check.abra-ca-dabra.app/check/guide/readiness) is the matching page. The [assessment](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment) is the longer note. The [questionnaire](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment-questionnaire) is the question list.

## What the bands mean

The Readiness Check scores 0 to 39 as Early, 40 to 59 as Forming, 60 to 79 as Ready, and 80 to 100 as Running.[^check] The check educates and routes. It does not certify a business.

<!--image:2-->

Gartner said in February 2025 that 63% of respondents do not have, or are unsure they have, the data management practices AI needs.[^gartner-data] Through 2026, organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects that lack AI-ready data. A framework that scores data as running while the source of truth is a shared inbox is scoring a wish.

Cisco's 2025 index surveyed 8,000 senior leaders in organizations with more than 500 employees.[^cisco] Pacesetters stayed near 13%. Those organizations were 4 times more likely to move a pilot into production. That sample is not a small business. The pattern still matters: production follows a written process, not a demo.

<!--cta:inline-->

Score the four groups in words first. Then run the check if you want the band.
