An AI readiness assessment asks whether a business can put a tool to work. Public indexes and checklists answer that for different audiences. This note compares them, then states what the [Readiness Check guide](https://check.abra-ca-dabra.app/check/guide/readiness) scores.

## What the public tools measure

Cisco's third annual AI Readiness Index, released 14 October 2025, surveyed 8,000 senior leaders in organizations with more than 500 employees, across 30 markets and 26 industries.[^cisco] Pacesetters stayed near 13% for three years. Those organizations were 4 times more likely to move a pilot into production, and 50% more likely to see measurable value. 83% of respondents plan to deploy AI agents, and nearly 40% expect agents to work alongside employees within a year. That sample is not a small-business survey.

Gartner reported on 26 February 2025 that 63% of respondents do not have, or are unsure they have, the data management practices AI needs.[^gartner] The same note predicts that through 2026 organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects that lack AI-ready data.

The OECD SME AI adoption tool, part of the 2026 French Group of Seven Presidency work, is a short self-check for smaller firms.[^oecd] It takes about five minutes, stores no data, and keeps answers in the browser. As of May 2026 it is a pilot, with preliminary validation by Group of Seven governments. It does not publish an adoption rate.

AWS published an AI readiness checklist in September 2025.[^aws] It walks five steps and sorts a business into four stages: Not yet ready, Emerging, Operational, and Scaling. The stages cover data, the use case, the stack, governance, and the metrics you will watch. The page does not cite an adoption percentage.

The Readiness Check is the one built for the firm in the room. It educates and routes. It does not diagnose. [Christopher Buzny](/about/christopher-buzny) publishes the method on the [studio site](/).

## What each band means

The check scores from 0 to 100. Bands are Early (0–39), Forming (40–59), Ready (60–79), and Running (80–100).[^check]

- Early: The work that matters still lives in people's heads. The first useful move is to write one workflow down and name who owns it.
- Forming: Pieces of the work are written down. A named owner and one system of record are what turn that into something a team can run.
- Ready: The business can put a new system to work. The open question is which workflow to start with, and who owns it day to day.
- Running: A real workflow already uses the tools. The next gain is the job that still waits on one person.

<!--image:3-->

A band is a state, not a grade. The four scores behind it are data, process, people, and the decision. The check takes five to seven minutes, one question per screen, and returns three suggestions for each section.
