An AI consultant for a small business should be able to say which job changes, which data it needs, and who can stop the work. If an off-the-shelf product already does that job, the honest answer is to buy the product. The [studio](/) works that way. The [agency comparison](/blog/ai-automation-agency-vs-consultant) is the hiring split. The [stall note](/blog/why-ai-pilots-stall) is what happens when the proof never becomes a process.

## What gets canceled

Gartner said in June 2025 that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027, citing cost, unclear business value, and inadequate risk controls.[^gartner-agentic] In a poll of 3,412 respondents, 19% said their organization had made significant investments in agentic AI, 42% had made conservative investments, 8% had made none, and 31% were waiting or unsure. Gartner also said only about 130 vendors offered what it considered real agentic products.

<!--image:2-->

McKinsey's 2025 survey puts the same gap in different numbers. 88% report regular AI use in at least one function, up from 78% a year earlier.[^mckinsey] 23% are scaling an agentic system somewhere, and 39% are experimenting. A consultant who quotes only the first figure is describing use, not a finished system.

<!--cta:inline-->

Red flags are specific. Guaranteed rankings ignore Google's guide, which says generative features are still SEO.[^google] No data plan means the source of truth was never named. No handover means the business cannot run the thing after the invoice.
