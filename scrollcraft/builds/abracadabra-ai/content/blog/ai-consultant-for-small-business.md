A useful AI consultant for a small business can name the job, the data, and the person who is allowed to stop the work. If an off-the-shelf product already does that job, the honest sentence is to buy the product and skip the custom project. We would rather lose the engagement than invent a build to justify a fee. That is how the [studio](/) works. The hiring split, consultant versus a firm that wants to own the install, is the [agency comparison](/blog/ai-automation-agency-vs-consultant). What happens when a pretty proof never becomes a process is the [stall note](/blog/why-ai-pilots-stall).

Bring the person who does the job into the first meeting. An owner alone will be sold a platform. The office manager, the bookkeeper, or the dispatcher will ask where the file lives, and that question is worth more than the slide with the architecture. If the consultant looks annoyed by it, you have learned something cheap.

We are opinionated about this because we have watched the alternative. A pitch that starts with a tool name has skipped the work. You can always get back to a tool. You cannot easily get back the month you spent implementing the wrong promise.

## What gets canceled

Gartner said in June 2025 that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027, citing cost, unclear business value, and inadequate risk controls.[^gartner-agentic] In a poll of 3,412 respondents, 19% said their organization had made significant investments in agentic AI, 42% had made conservative investments, 8% had made none, and 31% were waiting or unsure. Gartner also said only about 130 vendors offered what it considered real agentic products. Sit with that last line before you let a proposal use the word agent as decoration. The label is common. The thing Gartner was willing to call real was not.

<!--image:2-->

McKinsey's 2025 survey puts the same gap in different numbers. 88% report regular AI use in at least one function, up from 78% a year earlier.[^mckinsey] 23% are scaling an agentic system somewhere, and 39% are experimenting. A consultant who quotes only the first figure is describing use, not a finished system. Ask them to say the second pair of figures without flinching. If they cannot, they are selling the feeling of being current.

<!--cta:inline-->

Red flags are specific. Guaranteed rankings ignore Google's guide, which says generative features are still SEO.[^google] Nobody can guarantee a ranking in an overview, and a person who does is telling you they will also bluff the rest of the engagement. No data plan means the source of truth was never named. No handover means the business cannot run the thing after the invoice. We treat those three as reasons to end the meeting, not as issues to "workshop."

## A worked example

Sit in on a hiring conversation at a small clinic. Again, this is a walk-through we use, not a scoreboard. The owner-dentist wants help. The office manager, who actually runs recall and the front desk, is in the room because we asked her to be. The bookkeeper is not there yet. She will matter when the invoice arrives, and she should see the proposal before anyone signs.

The first consultant opens a laptop and names a platform. He talks about agents that will "handle the patient journey." He has not asked where the appointment book lives. He has not asked about the paper list of people who do not want texts. When the office manager asks who checks a message before it goes out, he says the system has guardrails. Guardrails are not a name. She writes nothing down. That is the right instinct.

The second consultant does not open a tool. She asks the office manager to describe last Monday, including the part that went wrong. The wrong part was a reminder that went to a patient who was already in the chair for treatment, because the recall list and the treatment list do not share a brain. The consultant writes a sentence: draft cleaning reminders from the appointment record only, and the office manager sends them. She asks where the do-not-contact names live. She asks what the manager should do when she is out. She says, out loud, that if a shelf product already drafts that reminder inside the practice software, they should buy that and stop talking to her.

We would hire the second person, and we would still make her put the stop rule in the proposal. Keep going only if the manager can clear the queue in the time they hold on the calendar. Stop if the drafts keep choosing the wrong patients. A consultant who will not write the stop is planning to linger. Lingering is how a small fee becomes the kind of project Gartner expects a large share of agentic work to cancel.

Notice who did the real interview. It was the office manager, not the owner. The owner felt the pain. The manager knows the drawer with the paper list. A good consultant will talk to the owner about money and risk, and will talk to the manager about the Monday. A weak one will charm the owner and avoid the manager, because the manager asks questions that shrink the proposal. Let the proposal shrink. A smaller true job is a better buy than a large foggy one.

## How we'd run it

We run a hiring conversation as a filter. The consultant is not owed a second meeting.

First, we send the questions ahead, in writing, so nobody can claim they were ambushed. What job will change. Where does the data live. Who checks the output, by name. How will we know it failed. What will you refuse to build. If the reply is a calendar link and a deck, and none of those answers, we do not take the meeting. Politeness is not a reason to donate an hour.

Second, we hold the meeting with the person who does the work, not only the owner. We ask the consultant to restate the job in one sentence the staff can repeat. If the sentence contains a tool name and no verb the staff recognize, we send them back. "Implement the platform" is not a job. "Draft the cleaning reminder and wait" is a job.

Third, we ask the buy-versus-build question directly, and we watch the face. If an off-the-shelf product already does this, will you say so. The honest answer is yes, and then a short list of what they would still help with, such as the wording and the checker. The dishonest answer is a custom build that recreates a feature the practice software already sells. We have no patience for that recreation. It flatters the consultant's hours and leaves the manager with two systems.

Fourth, we demand a handover before we talk about price. Who can run this when you leave. Where the rejects are written. What the bookkeeper will see on the invoice, in words, not only in a total. A price with no handover is a subscription to a person. That can be fine for advice. It is a bad way to buy a system. The [agency comparison](/blog/ai-automation-agency-vs-consultant) is the page we use when the owner is confused about which kind of help they even want.

Fifth, we check the red flags in the proposal language itself. Any promise of guaranteed rankings gets a no, because Google's own guide says generative features still depend on ordinary SEO. Any slide that quotes use without the scaling gap gets a question. Any plan that cannot name a risk control gets a no, which is the same reason Gartner gave for cancellation: cost, unclear business value, and inadequate risk controls. We would rather be known as difficult in the first meeting than stuck in the project that gets canceled after the staff have already changed their week around it.

## Common mistakes

Hiring the best talker is the ordinary mistake, and it is human. The owner is busy and a clear voice feels like competence. Competence, in this work, sounds slower. It sounds like questions about a drawer full of paper. If you leave a meeting energized and unable to repeat the job sentence, you were entertained.

Leaving the operator out of the room is the structural mistake. The office manager will inherit the queue. If she meets the system on launch day, she will route around it, and she will be right. Invite her while the proposal can still change. If the consultant objects, that objection is the finding.

Accepting "agent" as a product category is how the poll gets ignored. In that Gartner poll of 3,412 respondents, 19% had made significant investments in agentic AI, 42% conservative investments, 8% none, and 31% were waiting or unsure. Only about 130 vendors met Gartner's bar for a real agentic product. A proposal that calls a reminder draft an agent is borrowing a word. Ask what the software does on a Monday. If the answer is "it drafts and a person sends," call it a draft. The plainer word will get you a plainer price.

Skipping the search-and-ranking fantasy is a mistake in the other direction too, when the consultant brings it unasked. Guaranteed placement in an AI overview is a red flag even if you never asked for marketing help. It tells you the rest of the claims are flexible. Google's guide is public. A person selling certainty against a public guide will sell certainty against your files as well.

Signing because the quote is small is the last trap. A small quote with no stop rule becomes a long quote. Cost is one of the reasons those projects get canceled. Get the stop in writing while the number still feels harmless.

## What we'd do

We would tell a lot of owners to buy the shelf product and send the consultant home. That is the opinion that loses us work, and we still hold it. Custom work is justified when the job is specific to your files and a product will not touch them, or when the value is the thinking and the handover rather than the code. It is not justified because a platform demo was impressive and the office manager was not in the room to object.

We would also quote the awkward survey figures in the proposal review, on purpose. Over 40% of agentic AI projects canceled by the end of 2027 is not a reason to avoid all help. It is a reason to demand a value you can point at and a risk control with a name. The McKinsey gap belongs in the same conversation. 88% using AI in at least one function, up from 78% a year earlier, will be in the consultant's mouth. 23% scaling an agentic system, and 39% experimenting, should be in yours. If they only brought the first number, they are describing a hobby and pricing it like a system.

If the proposal mentions search, rankings, or "being the answer" in a generative result, we stop and open Google's guide with them.[^google] Generative features are still SEO. Nobody serious guarantees the outcome. And if the proof they show you cannot survive a normal week, read the [stall note](/blog/why-ai-pilots-stall) before you fund a sequel. The [studio](/) will say buy the product when that is the truth. We think you should require the same sentence from anyone else you pay.
