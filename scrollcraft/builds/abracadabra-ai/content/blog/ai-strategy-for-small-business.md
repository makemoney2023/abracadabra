An AI strategy for a small business is a short list: the job that changes, the data it needs, and the person who checks the output. It is not a tool catalog. The [assessment](/blog/ai-readiness-assessment) scores the same groups. The [implementation framework](/blog/ai-implementation-framework) is the sequence after the job is named. The [readiness framework](/blog/ai-readiness-framework) is the scoring sheet.

## Use is ahead of the plan

Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index says 75% of knowledge workers use AI at work.[^microsoft] 46% of those users started less than six months before the survey. 60% of leaders worry their organization's leadership lacks a plan and vision to implement AI. 78% of AI users bring their own tools, and that share is 80% at small and midsize businesses. 55% of leaders are concerned about talent. Only 25% of companies were planning to offer generative AI training.

NIST's risk framework groups the work as Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage.[^nist] A strategy that cannot point at those jobs has not started.

<!--image:2-->

Salesforce surveyed 3,350 leaders at firms of 200 employees and under.[^salesforce] The fieldwork ran through August and September 2024. 75% said they were at least experimenting with AI. Among growing small and midsize businesses, 83% said that. 78% of growing firms planned to increase AI investment, against 55% of firms that were declining. 91% of those already using AI said it boosts revenue.

<!--cta:inline-->

Those figures describe different populations. Microsoft asked knowledge workers and leaders. Salesforce asked small-business leaders. Neither figure is a substitute for the job you can name on one page.
